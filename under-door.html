Under-door lever manipulation

A purpose-built tool — or a bent wire coat hanger — slides through the gap at the base of a door and hooks the interior lever handle. The attacker pulls down. The door opens. The access control system logs nothing, because no credential was ever read.

Attack tool
Loider / wire / film (~$0–80)
Time to execute
5–15 seconds
Skill required
Minimal
Detection risk
Low (no event generated)
Alert generated
Door forced alarm only

How the weakness works

Most commercial and office doors are secured with an electronic access control system on the exterior — a card reader or keypad — while the interior side has a simple lever handle or push bar that anyone inside can use to exit. This design is correct for life safety: people always need to be able to exit freely.

The vulnerability is the door gap. Standard doors have a gap of ½ to 1 inch at the bottom. A thin, rigid tool — purpose-built or improvised — can pass through that gap, extend upward inside the secured space, hook around the lever handle, and depress it. The latch retracts. The door opens.

No credential is ever presented, so the access control system has nothing to log. If the door has a forced-door alarm (monitoring the door contact), it may fire — but only after the latch is already depressed and the door begins to open. In practice, many door contacts are set with a delay or are ignored after hours.

01Attacker confirms the door uses a lever handle or push bar on the interior, and has a visible gap at the base. Most hollow metal frames in commercial buildings qualify.
02Tool is inserted flat through the door gap. Purpose-built "loid" tools use a flexible stem with a hook end — improvised versions use wire or a coat hanger bent to shape.
03Tool is positioned against the interior lever handle and rotated or pulled downward. The lever depresses, retracting the latch bolt.
04Door swings open from the outside. No badge event, no REX event. Only a door-open alarm fires — if one is configured and monitored.
Field note: This technique works on the majority of commercial doors in US corporate environments. The only reliable real-time detection is camera coverage with alert logic. Recessed lever handles and door bottoms significantly slow the attack but rarely eliminate it entirely.
How it happens Door secured
Door is secured. Electronic latch holds on exterior; interior has a standard lever handle accessible from inside only — or so the design assumes.
Mitigation framework
How to defend against this

Gap elimination and lever protection are the most effective controls. Hardware upgrades remove the underlying vulnerability entirely.

Category:
Door gap elimination
Physical

Remove or reduce the gap at the base of the door so that a tool cannot be inserted at all, or is too restricted to maneuver.

Install door bottom sweeps that compress against the floor when the door closes — Pemko and NGP make commercial-grade versions
Add an automatic drop seal (rises when door opens, seals when closed) for high-security applications
Install a door frame astragal on the door edge to eliminate the gap between door and frame on the latch side
Threshold seals at the floor line further restrict insertion angle
Lever handle protection
Physical

If the gap cannot be fully eliminated, protect the lever handle from being engaged by a tool inserted from outside.

Install a lever wrap or lever restrictor that physically blocks the lever from being actuated by an external hook
Recess the lever into a mortise housing — reduces how much of the lever is accessible to an angled tool
Replace levers with knobs (harder to hook) where life safety egress requirements permit
Install a door-side guard plate on the interior covering the gap adjacent to the lever
Hardware upgrade
Technology

Replace the interior egress hardware with systems that cannot be actuated by an under-door tool.

Concealed vertical rod panic hardware (Von Duprin 98/99 series) routes the latch mechanism through the door itself — no exposed lever to hook
Electric latch retraction (ELR) hardware moves the unlocking mechanism to the credential side — interior always free, no hookable latch
Surface-applied vertical rod devices extend latch points to top and bottom of door — hooking one lever doesn't release the door
Electrified mortise locks where the entire latch/deadbolt system is credential-controlled
Detection and alerting
Detection

When hardware fixes are not immediately possible, ensure the attack is detected and responded to quickly.

Configure immediate forced-door alarms with short or zero delay — attacker gains entry at the moment the door opens
Camera coverage at door base level; low-angle cameras or fisheye lenses capture tool insertion attempts
Door contact monitoring with GSOC alert — any unexpected open event after hours triggers immediate review
Video analytics (motion at door perimeter) as an early-warning layer before the door opens
