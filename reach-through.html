CPSC-006 Critical Physical hardening Hardware upgrade

Reach-through thumb-turn bypass

A door can be locked with a solid deadbolt and still open in seconds if an attacker can reach the inside of it. A hooked tool slipped through a mail slot, a gap at the door edge, or a pane of broken or unsecured glass reaches in and simply turns the interior thumb-turn, works the lever, or presses the egress release. The bolt was never the weak part. The reach to it was.

Attack tool
J-tool / wire / reach rod (~$0–40)
Time to execute
5–30 seconds
Skill required
Minimal
Detection risk
Low (no forced entry)
Alert generated
Door-forced alarm only, if armed

How the weakness works

Many doors are secured on the outside by a lock with no key on the inside, just a thumb-turn, a lever, or a push pad, so that anyone inside can always get out. That is correct for life safety. The problem appears when the inside of the door is reachable from the outside through some opening near the hardware.

The usual openings are a mail slot or letter plate in the door, a wide gap at the door edge or between a pair of doors, a glass pane or sidelite next to the lock, or a transom or louver. A stiff wire, a bent rod, or a purpose-built reach tool goes through the opening, bends toward the hardware, and does exactly what a hand on the inside would do: rotate the thumb-turn to withdraw the deadbolt, pull the lever, or press the paddle of an exit device.

No credential is presented and nothing is forced, so the access control system logs nothing and, unless a door-position contact is armed and monitored, no alarm fires until the door is already open. The lock did its job. The opening beside it undid it.

01Attacker finds a locked door with a reachable opening near the hardware: a mail slot, a wide edge or center gap, a glass pane, a louver, or a transom.
02A hooked tool or stiff wire is fed through the opening and bent toward the interior thumb-turn, lever, or exit-device paddle.
03The tool rotates the thumb-turn or works the release, withdrawing the deadbolt or retracting the latch exactly as a person inside would.
04The door opens from the outside. No badge event, no forced entry. Only a door-position alarm can catch it, and only if one is armed and monitored.
Field note: The presence of a good deadbolt on a door with a mail slot or a glass pane beside the lock is a false comfort. Assume that any hardware within reach of an opening can be operated from the outside, and either move the hardware, close the opening, or take the free interior turn out of the lock.

Mitigation framework
How to defend against this

Four layers, strongest first. Taking the free interior turn out of the lock ends the attack, closing the opening denies the tool its reach, guarding the hardware makes it hard to grab, and detection catches what is left. Any one door usually needs two of these together.

Category:
Remove the thumb-turn from the attack
Hardware

If there is no free interior turn to grab, a tool that reaches the hardware has nothing to operate. This is the durable fix. Watch egress, since people inside must still get out.

Where the occupancy allows it, fit a double-cylinder deadbolt that is keyed on both sides, so there is no thumb-turn to rotate from outside.
Use a captive or lockable thumb-turn that can be keyed out of service, so it cannot be turned by a reached tool when the space is unoccupied.
On access-controlled doors, release the bolt on a valid credential rather than an interior turn, so there is no manual piece to reach.
Keep egress code-compliant by pairing an electric lock with a listed push-to-exit button, or a touchless wave-to-exit sensor, so people inside still leave in a single motion. Mount it well back from any slot, gap, or glass so the release itself cannot be reached or waved from outside.
Confirm the choice against your egress and fire code first. A double cylinder must not trap occupants; many occupancies require free egress and a captive emergency key instead.
Close the opening the tool reaches through
Physical

No reachable opening near the hardware means no path to the interior turn. Close every route: the slot, the edge gap, the center gap, and the glass.

Remove a mail slot from a secured door, or fit an interior letter-plate cowl or blocker so a tool cannot be angled from the slot toward the lock.
On a pair of doors, add an overlapping astragal or a removable center mullion so there is no gap at the meeting stile to reach through.
Close wide edge and undercut gaps with gasketing and sweeps, so a wire cannot be worked in beside the hardware (see CPSC-002).
Replace reachable glazing near the lock with laminated or security glass, or add a glazing guard, so a broken pane does not become the opening.
Guard and reposition the hardware
Physical

Where the turn and the opening both have to stay, make the hardware hard to grab and hard to line up on.

Fit a thumb-turn guard or shield that covers the turn so a hook cannot catch it.
Move the deadbolt and lever away from any slot, glass, or edge gap so a reached tool cannot line up on them.
Choose hardware that sits close to the door face and offers little for a hook to grab or lever against.
On exit devices, specify a design that resists being paddled or dogged from outside through a gap.
See it and alarm
Detection

Where prevention is not yet in place, make an attempt visible and loud in real time.

Arm the door-position contact and alarm on any opening with no matching badge or request-to-exit, so a credential-free open never clears silently.
Cover the door and its openings with a camera framed to catch a tool at the slot, gap, or glass, and alert on motion there after hours.
Keep the door-forced shunt short, since the attacker is inside the moment the bolt withdraws.
Route any unexpected after-hours opening at this door to the monitoring center as an event to investigate.
