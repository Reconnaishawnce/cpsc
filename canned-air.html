CPSC-001 Critical Sensor technology Physical hardening

REX sensor spoofing via compressed gas

A can of compressed air costs $10 and takes five seconds to use. In the right spot, it silently unlocks a maglock-secured door with no badge, no credential, and no forced entry. Most PIR-only REX sensors are vulnerable.

Attack tool
Compressed air can (~$10)
Time to execute
3–8 seconds
Skill required
None
Detection risk
Low (no alarm generated)
Alert generated
Valid REX event only

How the weakness works

Request-to-Exit (REX) sensors allow people to exit through a maglock-secured door without badging out. The most common type is a passive infrared (PIR) sensor, which detects changes in infrared radiation in its field of view. When a person's body heat enters the detection zone, the sensor sends a signal to the access control panel, which releases the maglock.

The vulnerability: PIR sensors can't distinguish between a human body and any other sudden source of infrared contrast. Compressed air or inert gas sprayed at the sensor creates a rapid temperature change that mimics the leading edge of a heat signature. The sensor triggers. The panel receives a valid REX event. The maglock releases. No alarm fires.

From the access control system's perspective, this is an entirely normal and legitimate exit event. It will appear in logs as a REX actuation, indistinguishable from a real person walking out unless you are looking at camera footage alongside the logs.

01 Attacker identifies door secured with a maglock. A small sensor device mounted near the door frame or above the door is the REX sensor, often with a small LED indicator.
02 Can of compressed air is positioned near the sensor. Distance and angle matter. The attacker may test from the door gap, or from the secured side if the sensor is visible.
03 A short burst of gas creates a rapid thermal contrast. The PIR sensor detects the change and fires a valid REX trigger event.
04 Access control panel logs a normal REX event. Maglock releases. Attacker opens the door. No alert generated.
Field note: In assessments, this technique reliably bypasses PIR-only REX sensors in under 10 seconds. The only consistent detection mechanism is camera footage reviewed alongside access logs, which almost no organization does in real time.
How it happens Normal operation
Normal operation: an authorized employee approaches the door. The PIR sensor detects their body heat, sends a valid REX signal, and the maglock releases.
Mitigation framework
How to defend against this

Four layers of control, strongest first. The durable fix is to remove the spoofable sensor from the trust path; the logic and detection changes are fast wins you can make today.

Category:
Take the sensor out of the trust path
Technology

The durable fix is to stop letting a device that a thermal change can trigger release a locked door. Strongest options first.

Use a mechanical request-to-exit switch built into the exit hardware (the RX switch in a push bar or panic device). A physical switch cannot be actuated remotely by gas or any other trick.
Where a maglock is not required, move to a fail-secure electric strike or electric latch retraction on an exit device, so egress no longer depends on a motion sensor at all.
If a motion sensor must stay, specify microwave (Doppler) or dual-technology detection that requires PIR and microwave to agree. A cold-gas burst satisfies neither.
On camera-covered doors, a video-verified REX requires visual confirmation of a person before it releases the lock.
Tighten the release logic
Policy / config

Settings you can change today on most panels and sensors, at no hardware cost.

Require a minimum activation dwell of about one to two seconds. A gas burst is a brief spike and will not hold like a person moving through the zone.
Where the sensor supports it, require sequential or dual-zone detection before the panel accepts the request-to-exit.
Alarm on any REX release that has no matching badge activity or occupancy, and on rapid repeated REX events at the same door.
Monitor the maglock bond sensor and the door position contact, so a release with no door-open, or a door-open with no valid REX, is flagged.
Put the sensor out of reach
Physical

Deny the attacker a line to the sensor and to the air right in front of it.

Mount the sensor on the interior ceiling, aimed down and inward, so it cannot be reached or sprayed from the insecure side or through the door gap.
Keep the detection zone off the door gap and the frame edge, so gas introduced at the gap falls outside the field of view.
Close the door undercut and perimeter gaps with sweeps and gasketing, so neither gas nor a tool can be worked in near the sensor (see CPSC-002).
Use a tamper-resistant housing and monitor the sensor tamper and supervision outputs.
See it and respond
Detection

If a spoof still gets through, make it loud and reviewable, ideally in real time.

Cover the REX side of every maglock door with a camera framed to capture anyone at the sensor or the door.
Correlate REX events with video and badge data: alert when a REX fires with no person on camera or no matching credential.
Auto-capture a short video clip on every after-hours REX for next-morning review.
Have the monitoring center treat an unexplained after-hours REX as an access event to investigate, not noise to clear.
