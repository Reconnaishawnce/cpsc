CPSC-001 Critical Sensor technology Physical hardening

REX sensor spoofing via compressed gas

A can of compressed air costs $10 and takes five seconds to use. In the right spot, it silently unlocks a maglock-secured door — no badge, no credential, no forced entry. Most PIR-only REX sensors are vulnerable.

Attack tool
Compressed air can (~$10)
Time to execute
3–8 seconds
Skill required
None
Detection risk
Low (no alarm generated)
Alert generated
Valid REX event only

How the weakness works

Request-to-Exit (REX) sensors allow people to exit through a maglock-secured door without badging out. The most common type is a passive infrared (PIR) sensor — it detects changes in infrared radiation in its field of view. When a person's body heat enters the detection zone, the sensor sends a signal to the access control panel, which releases the maglock.

The vulnerability: PIR sensors can't distinguish between a human body and any other sudden source of infrared contrast. Compressed air or inert gas sprayed at the sensor creates a rapid temperature change that mimics the leading edge of a heat signature. The sensor triggers. The panel receives a valid REX event. The maglock releases. No alarm fires.

From the access control system's perspective, this is an entirely normal and legitimate exit event. It will appear in logs as a REX actuation — indistinguishable from a real person walking out, unless you're looking at camera footage alongside the logs.

01 Attacker identifies door secured with a maglock. A small sensor device mounted near the door frame or above the door is the REX sensor — often with a small LED indicator.
02 Can of compressed air is positioned near the sensor. Distance and angle matter — attacker may test from the door gap or from the secured side if there's sensor visibility.
03 A short burst of gas creates a rapid thermal contrast. The PIR sensor detects the change and fires a valid REX trigger event.
04 Access control panel logs a normal REX event. Maglock releases. Attacker opens the door. No alert generated.
Field note: In assessments, this technique reliably bypasses PIR-only REX sensors in under 10 seconds. The only consistent detection mechanism is camera footage reviewed alongside access logs — which almost no organization does in real time.
How it happens Normal operation
Normal operation: an authorized employee approaches the door. The PIR sensor detects their body heat, sends a valid REX signal, and the maglock releases.
Mitigation framework
How to defend against this

Four categories of control, ranked by effectiveness. Sensor upgrades are the most reliable fix; configuration changes are fast and cost-nothing wins.

Category:
Sensor technology upgrade
Technology

Replace PIR-only sensors with multi-technology or active IR sensors that can't be fooled by a cold gas burst.

Dual-technology sensors combine PIR and microwave — both must trigger simultaneously to unlock
Active IR sensors transmit an IR beam; gas disruption is far less effective against active vs passive detection
DSP (Digital Signal Processing) sensors analyze signal waveform and reject anomalous thermal signatures
Video REX sensors integrate a camera; the system can optionally require camera verification before releasing
Configuration hardening
Policy / config

Adjust sensor and panel settings to reduce the attack window. Many controls are free within existing hardware.

Add a minimum signal duration (e.g. 2 seconds) — a gas burst spike is shorter than a human body dwell time
Reduce sensor sensitivity in areas adjacent to unsecured space
Configure alarm on rapid repeated REX events at the same reader (retry behavior)
Enable anti-mask detection where the sensor supports it
Physical hardening
Physical

Limit how easily an attacker can reach the sensor from the unsecured side of the door.

Recess the sensor higher on the wall, out of reach through the door gap
Install a tamper-resistant sensor housing or cage that limits cone accessibility
Reduce door gap with sweeps and astragals so the sensor cannot be reached through the gap
Relocate sensor to interior ceiling mount where it can't be targeted from the unsecured side
Detection and response
Detection

If prevention isn't possible, ensure the attack is detectable after the fact — and ideally in real time.

Mount a camera covering the REX side of the door, aligned to capture anyone near the sensor
Set up automatic video-clip capture triggered by REX events for after-hours review
Generate an alert when a REX event occurs with no corresponding badge-in on the same door
GSOC review of REX event logs — any access pattern during off-hours warrants camera review
